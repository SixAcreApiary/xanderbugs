Putting the Raised Beds to Sleep for Winter

By the backyard crew · 6 min read

The tomatoes have given their last, the beans are crisp brown pods, and the mornings now carry that sharp smell of leaves turning. It is the right moment to tuck the raised beds in for the cold months so they wake up rich and loose in spring.

Clear out, but not too much

Pull spent annuals and anything that showed signs of disease, but leave a few hollow stems standing. They give shelter to the helpful insects that will patrol the beds next year. Chop the healthy leftovers and drop them back onto the soil as a light mulch.

Feed the soil, not the plants

Spread two or three inches of finished compost across each bed. There is no need to dig it in; the worms will do that work slowly over the winter. Top it with shredded leaves to keep the surface from crusting and to hold moisture in place.

Plant a cover crop

A quick sowing of winter rye or crimson clover protects the soil from heavy rain and adds organic matter when you turn it under in March. Scatter the seed by hand, rake it in lightly, and water once if the weather is dry.

Finally, clean and oil your hand tools before you hang them up. Future you, standing in the shed on the first warm day, will be grateful.
